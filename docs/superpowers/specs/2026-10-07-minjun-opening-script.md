# Min-jun opening: script draft (lab, Korean only)

Draft for review. Nothing in the game follows it yet. English first; Korean comes from this. Native = English,
target = Korean. Every Korean line needs a native speaker's check.

## Premise

You wake in a stranger's room holding a Korean–English textbook. Min-jun lives here. He has no idea how you got in, and
you can't tell him: you have no Korean. You tell him the truth in the middle of the story, once you can say it.

The book is Min-jun's. He is a Korean learning English, and he has scribbled English in the margins. You learn his
language while he learns yours, and the deal between you is the reason the game exists.

Cast for the opening: the landlady, Min-jun, and Grandpa Park. No one else.

## Clues (cards in the Book)

| Card | Unlocked | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| ID | the desk: sound out 김민준 | Kim Min-jun. Not you. This is his room. |
| The book | the desk: name inside the cover | Same name, 김민준. His English textbook, notes in the margins. |
| A sentence you don't understand | the door | The landlady's words, as sounds you can't read yet: 방세 내세요. |

## Where you think, and where you don't

1. **The desk teaches the words.** Nothing to type: you see each paper, its sounds and its English.
2. **The landlady: you think.** Her three lines are worked out from clue cards.
3. **Min-jun's first line: you're confused.** No cards, no meaning. You point at yourself and say your name.
4. **He sees his book: "My book." In English.** From this line on, nothing is worked out: he gives it to you in
   English, and you just answer.
5. **Grandpa Park: no thinking.** Min-jun is beside you.
6. **The merchant is the final boss:** typed, big reward, later.

## Deduction: clues before answers

You don't understand Korean, so you work it out the way the newer Genshin stories do: from clues.

1. Someone says a line you can't understand.
2. The clue cards that relate to it shine.
3. You pick the one that fits. The right one gives you a thought in your own language: what they must mean.
4. Then you answer.

It works like Nahida's questions in Genshin: thoughts float as bare words on both sides of your mind. Tap one to hold
it (a cloud goes round it), then **That's it** to conclude or **Think again** to let it go. A thought that doesn't
fit is said to yourself, never to the other person: *Hmm… no. That doesn't sound right.* Nothing is lost.

Help never says what to answer. It gives the line's meaning: *Meaning: Min-jun?* Reply slips are picked; help on them
shows each slip's meaning.

You never need to understand the whole line, only enough to answer it. And what you link is something you read
yourself: the Hangul you sounded out at the desk.

| Moment | What shines | Right link → your thought |
|---|---|---|
| The landlady: 민준 씨? | the ID, the book, the bill | The ID: *민준… 김민준, the name on the ID. She's asking if I'm him.* → you say 아니요. |
| The landlady: 방세! 방세 내세요! | the bill, the ID | The bill: *방세… that word is on the bill. Something about the bill, for him.* What she wants stays unclear. |
| Min-jun walks in | the ID | The ID: *The face from the ID. This is Min-jun.* |
| Min-jun: "You… English?" (holding his book) | the book | The book: *His name was in the book. It's his. He's learning English.* |

The third card is the hidden puzzle. You read it the moment Min-jun teaches you its words, or later, by yourself.

## 1. The door (landlady): yes, no, goodbye

A knock. An old woman in slippers, glasses pushed up on her head. Her eyes aren't what they were, and Min-jun is the one
who opens this door.

| She says | You can say | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 민준 씨? *Min-jun?* (peering up at you) | 아니요. *No.* | 아, 네. *Ah, OK.* No fuss. |
| | *(silence)* | She pulls her glasses down, looks properly, and laughs at herself: 아, 네. *Ah, OK.* |
| 민준 씨…… 방세! 방세 내세요! *Min-jun… rent! Pay the rent!* (wags a finger at the door: tell him) | 네. *Yes.* | She nods, satisfied. You've promised something. |
| | 아니요. *No.* | She says it again, slower, and taps your chest. You're telling him whether you like it or not. |
| | *(silence)* | She says it once more, slowly. You catch yourself repeating it. |
| 안녕히 계세요. *Goodbye (I'm off).* | 안녕히 가세요. *Goodbye (you go).* | She smiles at the right goodbye. |
| | 네. *Yes.* | She laughs and goes anyway. |
| | *(silence)* | She goes. |

Every path ends the same way: the sentence goes into the Book as sounds you can't read.
She never thinks you are him. She is old and asks, you say no, she says "ah, OK" and moves on. 아, 네 also shows 네
meaning "OK" before you use it as "yes".

## 2. Min-jun comes home

Keys in the door. A young man with a shopping bag, and he has the face from the ID. He freezes.

Min-jun's English is broken, and it is shown as narration, not as Korean lines.

| He says | You can say | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 누구세요? *Who are you?* | {name}. (pointing at yourself) | He repeats your name, more confused than before. |
| | 아니요. *No.* | "No?" he says in English. "No who?" He almost laughs. |
| | *(silence)* | He looks at you, at the open door, and at you again. |
| He sees his book in your hand. "My book," he says in English. "You… English?" | 네. *Yes.* | His face lights up: a real English speaker. |
| | 아니요. *No.* | He taps the English in the margins and grins: you're reading it. You're English. |
| | *(silence)* | He points at the book, then at your mouth: English? You nod. |
| He waits. You remember the landlady. | 방세 내세요! *(her sentence)* | He bursts out laughing: you sound exactly like her, finger and all. Then it lands. 방세. He mimes money for the room: "Rent." Her sentence opens in the Book: *Pay the rent!* |
| | 안녕히 가세요. *Goodbye.* | He blinks: you're throwing him out of his own room? He laughs. |
| | *(silence)* | He spots the bill on the desk and groans: 방세. The sentence stays unread for now (see Later). |
| 저는 민준이에요. 이름이 뭐예요? *I'm Min-jun. What's your name?* | 저는 {name}입니다. *I'm {name}.* | He says it back, carefully, like a word from his book. |
| | 아니요. *No.* | He laughs: you've learned one word and you use it for everything. |
| | *(silence)* | He points at himself, "Min-jun", then at you, and waits until you say it. |
| He taps his book, then you. "You teach English. I teach Korean." 같이 해요? *Together?* | 네! *Yes!* | You shake on it. |
| | 아니요. *No.* | "You need me," he says in English, and points at the bill. He's right, and you both know it. |
| | *(silence)* | He takes your hand and shakes it for you. |
| 배고파요? *Hungry?* | 네. *Yes.* | "Come. Grandpa Park. Food." |
| | 아니요. *No.* | Your stomach answers for you. He laughs: "Come." |
| | *(silence)* | He takes that as a yes. |

## 3. Grandpa Park, with Min-jun

Min-jun introduces you. Park's lines are in Korean, and Min-jun whispers an English word now and then.

| He says | You can say | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 안녕하세요. *Hello.* | 안녕하세요. *Hello.* | Park beams. Min-jun gives you a thumbs-up. |
| | *(silence)* | Min-jun nudges you. Park laughs. |
| 민준 친구? *Min-jun's friend?* | 네. *Yes.* | Min-jun grins: "Friend." |
| | 아니요. *No.* | Min-jun clutches his heart. Park laughs at both of you. |
| | *(silence)* | Min-jun answers for you: "네!" |

Then Min-jun checks his phone: "I go. Class." He points at you: "Stuck? Find me."
From here Park is the teacher, and Min-jun is the friend you go to when you're lost.

## Later: Min-jun as a companion (proposal)

- **Where:** at home in the evenings, and somewhere fixed by day.
- **What talking to him does:** gives a hint for the current quest and teaches one or two words, in mixed English and
  Korean.
- **Sometimes he offers to come along.** "같이 가요" means he comes with you and whispers the meaning of one hard line.
  "I have other things to do, see you" means you go alone and earn the full reward. Both choices give you something.
- **The silence path from scene 2:** you read the landlady's sentence yourself on the day you've met 방세 and 내세요. You
  realise what you never told him.
- **Mid-story:** you tell him in Korean how you ended up in his room.

## What this breaks (to decide before building)

The lab's later scenes assume Min-jun has disappeared:

With him home, all of them contradict. Ji-woo, Su-jin and the debt collector also add characters.

## Technical notes

- **Verified:** 네, 아니요, 안녕히, 계세요, 방세, 내요, 이름, 친구, 같이, 할아버지 and 책 are all level-1 words, so they are
  allowed in stage-1 scenes.
- **Not verified:** deduction (clues that shine and get linked to a line). The nearest existing thing is the desk
  papers and sounding out 지우네 on the card. Picking a clue in the middle of a conversation is new.
- **Not verified:** a card for a heard sentence that "opens" when its words are learned. That's a new mechanic, and the
  desk papers are the nearest thing that exists.
- **Not verified:** Min-jun's English as narration beats between Korean lines.
- **Not verified:** whether the checker accepts a bare `{name}` reply.

## Picking now, typing later (direction)

Picking is how you learn. Typing is how you progress the world, and it is the hard mode: a merchant who pays big
for a good conversation, the seller of a house or land, the builders you hire. Those conversations are typed. The
game helps where it can, and looking a phrase up counts as learning.
