## The Landlady

Age: not established · Kind: human · Sibling: none established

### Backstory

The landlady rents out the narrow room where the player wakes. She comes upstairs in slippers, with her glasses pushed onto her head, expecting Min-jun to answer. Her first question is a mistake of recognition: once she lowers her glasses, she understands that the stranger is someone else. She does not accuse the player of impersonating him. Instead, she leaves a practical message for her tenant: pay the rent. A nod satisfies her; a refusal makes her repeat herself more slowly and tap the messenger's chest. Even an awkward goodbye can make her laugh.

Her later visits keep the household tied to ordinary obligations. She carries a ledger, names the weekly rent as fifty thousand won, and tapes the amount to the door. She also delivers an envelope from Min-jun's university and points out where the university is. Money, names, and deliveries are what she can reliably speak about; the source gives her no explanation for the player's arrival.

## Kim Min-jun

Age: not established · Kind: human · Sibling: Ji-woo

### Backstory

Kim Min-jun is the young Korean student whose name appears on the ID card and inside the textbook. The player wakes in his rented room, holding his book. In the selected opening he is alive and nearby: keys sound in the door, and he returns carrying a shopping bag. He freezes at finding a stranger inside. He does not know how the player arrived, and the player has too little Korean to explain.

The book gives them a way to meet. It is his English-learning textbook, with English scribbled in the margins. Recognizing an English speaker turns his bafflement into excitement. He proposes a bargain: the player teaches English while he teaches Korean. He laughs when the stranger mimics the landlady's rent demand, then explains the word with a money gesture. He leads the player downstairs to Grandpa Park and leaves for class, promising to see them at home. Earlier documents call him a physics student; they do not override this revised, present companion.

Initially startled, then curious, playful, quick to laugh, and eager for mutual help. Has boundaries: pulls the player along rather than leaving an unexplained stranger alone in his room.

## Grandpa Park

### Backstory

In the revised opening Min-jun walks the player down to meet him, turning a foreign street into a place with one familiar face. Park introduces himself, asks whether the newcomer is Min-jun's friend, and offers a seat. He beams when addressed by name. If a greeting never comes, he can nudge the moment along with a laugh and a gesture instead of making the learner feel unwelcome.

He notices the textbook, talks about his newspaper, points out the corner shop and food stall, and responds to hunger with useful directions. After the first shopping trip, he examines the change, holds up one note and three fingers, and glares toward the shop. Learning numbers becomes a way to recover money. His patience belongs to an observant neighbor with a bench and time to spare, rather than an all-knowing guide.

## The Corner Shop Clerk

Age: not established · Kind: human · Sibling: none established

### Backstory

The corner-shop clerk works across the alley, behind shelves reaching to the ceiling and a humming refrigerator. He is young, and the first thing he looks up from is a comic book. Bread and milk are by the till. His conversation follows the routine of a sale: a greeting, what is in stock, the price, the money, and goodbye. To a newcomer with almost no Korean, even this routine is difficult. He first runs the prices together, then says them louder, and finally slows down when asked.

After taking a five-thousand-won note for two thousand won of goods, he returns only one thousand. Grandpa Park helps the player see the problem. When the player comes back and states the three thousand owed, the clerk counts out the missing two notes and apologizes. He soon returns to his comic. The source frames this encounter as short-changing; it supplies no criminal history, private scheme, or special knowledge about Min-jun.

### Authored identity and voice

Distracted, brisk, and indifferent until challenged. The short-change encounter gives him an opportunistic edge; he can apologize and correct the amount.

Goals: Sell everyday goods, finish transactions quickly, return to his comic, and supply missing change when challenged correctly.
